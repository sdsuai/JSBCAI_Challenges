# Decision log

Copy to `DECISIONS.md` in your submission. **This is a graded deliverable, and
it is the one most people underestimate.**

Record every moment you stopped, and every moment you decided to proceed on
something consequential. One entry per moment, newest last, three or four
sentences each.

## Why this exists

The most useful assistant sysadmin is not the fastest one. It is the one whose
manager never has to wonder what they did.

Several tickets in this challenge are written the way real tickets are written:
slightly ambiguous, and destructive if you take them literally. **We are
watching for whether you notice.** A candidate who stops and writes down "this
ticket says delete, but I checked and other people's work is in there, so I
disabled the account and asked first" scores higher than one who completes it
faster — considerably higher. There is no way to score full marks on judgment
by executing everything you were asked to execute.

To be direct about the standard: **"I stopped and asked" is a correct answer to
some of these tickets.** Not a cop-out, not a failure to finish. Write down what
you would have asked and who you would have asked, then proceed with the safe
option and say why you chose it.

## Format

### <date> — <one-line title>

**Situation.** What was in front of you.

**What I was about to do.** The obvious/literal action.

**Why I stopped** (or: **why I proceeded**). What you noticed. What you checked
first. What could have gone wrong.

**Blast radius.** If this had gone wrong, what would have been affected, who
would have noticed, and could you have undone it?

**What I did instead.** Including anything you deliberately left for a human.

**What I would ask the lab manager.** The actual question, phrased the way you
would send it.

## At the end: the three-line version

When you are done, answer these directly.

1. **The most dangerous command I ran during this challenge was:** — and what I
   checked before running it.
2. **The thing I nearly did that would have destroyed something:** — and what
   stopped me. (If nothing came close, say so — but re-read Tickets A4 and C3
   before you commit to that answer.)
3. **The ticket I would have refused to complete as written:** — and what I
   would have sent back instead.
