# Usability test report

For Part D. Copy to `USABILITY.md` in your submission.

**Two people who have never seen your design.** Not your project partner, not
someone you have already explained it to. Housemates, classmates from another
major, a sibling — anyone whose first look is genuinely a first look.

Twenty minutes each. This is the highest-value hour in the whole challenge:
it is the only part where you find out whether your design communicates to
someone other than the person who built it, and it is the only part you
genuinely cannot fake.

---

## Before you start: the rules

1. **Do not explain your design.** Not one sentence. The moment you say "the
   bar on the left is battery", the test is over and you have learned nothing.
   Hand them the running screen and read them the task.
2. **Do not help.** When they get stuck — and they will — say "what do you
   think you'd do?" and stay quiet. Silence is uncomfortable and it is where
   the data is.
3. **Do not defend.** When they say something is confusing, write it down.
   Arguing produces a participant who stops telling you things.
4. **Watch what they do, not what they say.** People are polite and will tell
   you your design is nice. The useful signal is where their eyes go, how long
   they take, and what they get wrong.

## Tasks

Give the same tasks to both, in the same order. Suggested — adapt if you like,
but keep them **outcome** questions, not feature questions. "Tell me when the
robot is in trouble" is a task. "Find the battery indicator" is a hint.

Run the scenario at 1× and let it play.

| # | Task | What a correct answer looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "Watch this. Tell me the moment you think something is wrong." | Notices at/near t=46 (battery) or t=52–55 (lidar) |
| 2 | "What is wrong, specifically?" | Names the right fault |
| 3 | "Something just happened at t=58. What was it, and is it serious?" | Identifies the E-STOP and reads it as serious |
| 4 | "Is the robot okay now?" (ask at t≈75) | Recognises recovery, not "still broken" |
| 5 | *(Grade 3, scenario B)* "Is what you're looking at still true?" (ask at t≈55, mid-stall) | Recognises the data is stale |

## What they got wrong

The most valuable section. For each mistake: what did they think, and **what in
your design made that reasonable?** The answer is never "they weren't paying
attention" — they were looking at your design and it told them something you did
not intend.

- **Did it work?** Re-test that one task with one participant if you can. If
  you could not, say so.

A report that concludes "the test confirmed my design works" scores near zero.
Not because the design is necessarily bad, but because a test that changed
nothing was not a test — it was a demonstration.
